# TManage — Primary Visual Reference Analysis

**Primary file:** `assets/references/TManage-primary-visual-reference.webp`  
**Secondary crop:** `assets/references/Design-System-3.webp`  
**Authority:** Visual north star for Claude design. It controls aesthetic DNA; product behavior, permissions, and content remain controlled by the product/security files.

## 1. What the image is doing

The reference is an editorial dashboard composition on a pale concrete canvas. A long black-and-acid-lime schedule capsule anchors the top. The second level uses a very large page wordmark/title, a compact black capsule action, and oversized metrics with tiny directional markers. Below, open filter capsules precede broad white content cards. One lime card creates the focal point. A dark summary/media panel overlaps the light canvas on the right, adding depth and a second mode without turning the whole product dark.

The design feels modern because scale, geometry, whitespace, and contrast are coordinated—not because it uses decorative effects.

## 2. Measured visual relationships to preserve

- The top track spans most of the available width and is visually stronger than conventional app chrome.
- Primary black and lime areas are large enough to be remembered.
- Page title and large figures are several times the size of ordinary labels.
- Cards are broad and low-to-medium height, with large radii and very little shadow.
- Circular controls repeat across navigation, card actions, media, and filters.
- White/pale-gray zones carry most information; lime is concentrated.
- The right dark panel occupies roughly a quarter of the product canvas and overlaps rather than dividing the page with a hard conventional column.
- Small avatars are clipped into circles and positioned where responsibility/participation matters.
- Negative space between title, metrics, filters, and content makes the composition feel editorial.

These are relational rules, not fixed pixels. They must adapt by surface and viewport.

## 4. TManage translations

### Top schedule → Pulse Track

The track shows human/time/state continuity. It can display today’s schedule, milestone position, request status, deliverable version progression, or client journey. The current segment is lime; ink can represent confirmed structure; outline represents future/uncertain.

### Lead cards → Work Cards

Replace people-as-sales-leads with work objects. Cards show type, title, owner, next action, due/forecast, status, and audience. One focal lime card represents the recommended/current item, not commercial “hotness.”

### Metrics → Delivery decisions

Use large figures for days to committed milestone, approval wait, blocked critical commitments, or forecast movement. Tiny arrows/markers have textual equivalents and open evidence.

### Summary panel → Context Lens / Meeting Summary

The dark layered panel becomes a contextual surface for source material, summary, decisions, files, and next actions. It can accompany a meeting, deliverable review, AI answer, or selected project object. It is dismissible/resizable and becomes a sheet on smaller screens.

### Left orbs → Orbital Rail

The circular controls form TManage’s top-level navigation. The active orb is ink; urgent/current can be lime. Labels, tooltips, focus, and expanded navigation preserve usability.

## 5. System invariants

- At least one of the following appears on any major product screen: Orbital Rail/Docked Orbs, Pulse Track, Focus Capsule, Audience Halo, Work Story, or dark Context Lens.
- No more than one dominant lime field per viewport, except connected Pulse Track segments.
- Black capsules are reserved for primary/decisive actions and structural anchors.
- Large type expresses hierarchy, not marketing everywhere.
- Operational density may reduce radius/scale but cannot switch to generic blue-gray SaaS styling.
- Depth comes from plane contrast, overlap, and outline before shadow or blur.

## 6. Required Claude analysis before design

Claude must attach the uploaded image to its own visual-reference analysis and explicitly identify palette, type, geometry, grid, whitespace, cards, capsule track, metrics, orbs, lime focal card, and dark overlap. It then creates TManage-specific style tiles and stress screens. A purely textual promise to “match the vibe” is insufficient.

## 7. Visual rejection examples

Reject a result that uses a navy sidebar, blue primary buttons, tiny 8–12 px radii, equal KPI cards, a gradient hero, low whitespace, uniform card grids, or a standard table everywhere. Also reject a superficial imitation that pastes lime onto an otherwise generic component library.

## 8. Validation views

Compare the reference beside: Home, Project Overview, Work List, Client Portal, Meeting Summary, and Automation Builder. Home/Project/Client should show strong direct DNA. Work List/Admin should show quieter inherited DNA through type, orbs, capsules, line, selection, and context panel. All six should look like one product.
